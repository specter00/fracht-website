Customs FAQ

Short answers to the questions that most often come up before a shipment, in both directions. Every statement has a source: customs and tax authorities, legal texts, the EU Commission, the International Chamber of Commerce and specialist sources, mostly in German. The answers do not replace customs or tax advice for the individual case.

For every shipment

Do I need an EORI number?

Yes, as soon as you submit a customs declaration, for export as well as for import. The EORI number is required for customs clearance in the EU and is stated in customs declarations.

The German customs administration (Generalzolldirektion) issues it free of charge on application. Applications go through the customs portal (www.zoll-portal.de); since 1 October 2026 using the portal is mandatory.

For export, small consignments up to EUR 1,000 can also be declared orally without an EORI number.

Sources
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): EORI-Nummer und Beantragung einer EORI-Nummer, accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Internetzollanmeldungen: Fragen und Antworten, accessed 16 Nov 2026

EXW, FOB, DAP or DDP: what do the delivery terms settle?

The Incoterms 2020 set out in the sales contract where the seller delivers and who bears costs and risk up to that point. Common terms are:

  • EXW (Ex Works): the buyer bears all costs from the named pickup place. The seller does not have to load the goods or clear them for export.
  • FCA (Free Carrier): the seller hands the goods to the carrier or another person named by the buyer and bears the risk up to that point.
  • FOB (Free on Board): the seller delivers on board the vessel named by the buyer. From then on the buyer bears risk and costs.
  • CIF: the seller concludes the contract of carriage and pays the freight to the port of destination. The risk, however, passes to the buyer once the goods are on board.
  • DAP (Delivered at Place): the seller delivers to the agreed place of destination. The buyer handles and pays for import clearance.
  • DDP (Delivered Duty Paid): the seller handles and pays for all customs formalities, for export and for import.

If you buy in China or India on EXW, FCA or FOB terms, for example, you bear the transport costs from that point. You can request the transport to Germany through this site.

The ICC points out that foreign sellers may have difficulty carrying out import clearance in the destination country themselves under DDP. Who pays the import VAT is not stated explicitly in the ICC overviews; settle this in the sales contract.

Sources
  • ICC Germany: Die Incoterms 2020 als Übersicht, accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • ICC Academy (Internationale Handelskammer): DAP & DDP Incoterms 2020 explained: Key differences (25.02.2025), accessed 16 Nov 2026

How is the chargeable weight calculated?

In air freight, the actual weight or the volumetric weight counts, whichever is higher. The volumetric weight is length × width × height in centimetres, divided by 6,000. The result is rounded up to the next 0.5 kilograms.

Sea freight groupage (LCL) is charged per cubic metre or by weight (W/M): 1,000 kilograms count as one cubic metre, the higher value counts.

Individual airlines and shipping lines can apply different rules. Use the rate estimator to work out the chargeable weight of your shipment.

Sources
  • ASEAN (Schulungsunterlage, zitiert IATA TACT Rules 3.9.4): Air Freight – Calculation of Air Freight, accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • Cogoport: How to calculate chargeable weight in ocean freight (26.02.2026), accessed 16 Nov 2026

Can cosmetics be dangerous goods in air freight?

Yes. The IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations list perfumery products, for example, under the number UN 1266.

Please state in your request whether your shipment contains perfume, sprays or nail polish. The classification is then cleared up before the offer.

Source
  • IATA: Dangerous Goods Regulations, 66th Edition, Addendum (30.04.2025), accessed 16 Nov 2026

Export from Germany

For shipments from Germany to countries outside the EU, for example to the Gulf, to China or to India. This page does not cover the import rules of China and India; please clarify them with your recipient.

When do I need an export declaration?

Shipments to a country outside the EU must be declared to customs for export, usually electronically through the IT system ATLAS-Ausfuhr.

  • Small consignments: if the shipment does not exceed a value of EUR 1,000 or a net mass of 1,000 kilograms, it can be declared orally directly at the customs office of exit.
  • Up to a goods value of EUR 3,000, the single-stage procedure is possible: the goods are presented and declared directly at the customs office of exit.
  • Above that, the two-stage procedure applies, with two customs offices: the customs office of export and the customs office of exit.

Splitting a shipment into partial shipments to stay below the value limit is not permitted. Whether export clearance in Germany is included is stated in the offer.

Sources
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Warenausfuhr im einstufigen Verfahren (sogenannte Kleinsendungen), accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Ausfuhrverfahren, accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Das IT-Verfahren ATLAS-Ausfuhr, accessed 16 Nov 2026

Who submits the export declaration?

The exporter is the person established in the EU who decides on the goods leaving the EU. The exporter or declarant must submit the customs declaration (§ 12 (2) AWV).

This power of decision can be transferred to another person established in the EU, for example a freight forwarder.

Source
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Ausführer, accessed 16 Nov 2026

What proof do I need for a VAT-exempt export delivery?

Each export declaration receives a registration number, the MRN (Master Reference Number). Once the goods have left the EU, the customs office of export sends the exit confirmation (Ausgangsvermerk) electronically.

With an electronic export declaration, the exit confirmation is the proof of export for VAT purposes (§ 6 UStG, §§ 9 and 10 UStDV). Keep it together with the invoice.

Sources
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Normales Verfahren (zweistufiges Ausfuhrverfahren), accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Das IT-Verfahren ATLAS-Ausfuhr, accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • Bundesministerium der Justiz, Gesetze im Internet: § 9 und § 10 UStDV: Ausfuhrnachweis bei Ausfuhrlieferungen, accessed 16 Nov 2026

Do I need to consider export controls?

Exports of dual-use goods require a licence if the goods are listed in Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2021/821 or in Part I B of the German export list. Check before shipping whether your goods are listed. Licences are issued by the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control (BAFA).

Source
  • Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle (BAFA): Exportkontrolle und das BAFA (Leitfaden, Stand November 2025), accessed 16 Nov 2026

Import into Germany

For goods from countries outside the EU, for example from China, India or the Emirates.

How are goods from a country outside the EU imported?

The goods must be declared to customs. The German customs administration handles customs procedures through the IT system ATLAS, which has a dedicated part for imports.

The EORI number is stated in the customs declaration. The commercial invoice is the basis for the customs value. If the shipment consists of several packages, the customs office can ask for a packing list, and also for transport documents.

Whether import clearance in Germany is included is stated in the offer.

Sources
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): ATLAS, accessed 18 Nov 2026
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): EORI-Nummer und Beantragung einer EORI-Nummer, accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Notwendige Unterlagen, accessed 17 Nov 2026

Do I have to declare the import myself?

No. You can appoint a customs representative (Art. 18 Union Customs Code), for example a freight forwarder or customs agent. There are two forms:

  • Direct representation: the representative acts in your name and on your behalf.
  • Indirect representation: the representative acts in their own name but on your behalf.
Source
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Vertretung, accessed 18 Nov 2026

How high is the customs duty?

That depends on the goods: each tariff number has its own duty rates. They can be looked up in the electronic tariff of the German customs authority (EZT-online) and in the EU database TARIC.

Please state the tariff number (HS code) in your request if you know it.

Source
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Zollsatz (Einfuhr aus einem Nicht-EU-Staat), accessed 18 Nov 2026

What is customs duty calculated on?

On the customs value. It is usually based on the transaction value, the price actually paid or payable for the goods.

Transport and insurance costs up to the place where the goods are brought into the EU are added to the price. If you buy ex works or FOB, duty is therefore also charged on the freight up to the EU border.

Sources
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Transaktionswert für die eingeführte Ware, accessed 18 Nov 2026
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Beförderungs- und Versicherungskosten sowie Lade- und Behandlungskosten (Berichtigungen nach Art. 71 UZK), accessed 18 Nov 2026

What is import VAT, and can I deduct it?

On import, import VAT (Einfuhrumsatzsteuer) is due in addition to customs duty. It is 19 percent, or 7 percent for goods listed in Annex 2 of the German VAT Act.

It is based on the customs value. Added to it are, among other things, the transport costs up to the first destination in the EU (§ 11 UStG).

Businesses can deduct the import VAT on goods imported for their business as input tax (§ 15 (1) no. 2 UStG).

Sources
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Zolltarifauskunft für Umsatzsteuerzwecke (Einfuhrumsatzsteuer), accessed 18 Nov 2026
  • Bundesministerium der Justiz, Gesetze im Internet: § 11 UStG: Bemessungsgrundlage für die Einfuhr, accessed 18 Nov 2026
  • Bundesministerium der Justiz, Gesetze im Internet: § 15 UStG: Vorsteuerabzug, accessed 18 Nov 2026

Are there lower duties for goods from India?

To apply preferential arrangements, customs asks for the corresponding documents. Under the EU's Generalised Scheme of Preferences (GSP), the proof of origin is a statement on origin that an exporter registered in the REX system adds to the invoice.

For India, GSP preferences are suspended for several product groups, from 2026 to 2028 for example for minerals, rubber and motor vehicles. Whether your goods qualify can be checked using the tariff number.

The trade agreement between the EU and India has been negotiated but is not yet in force (as of the EU Commission page of 11 September 2026).

Sources
  • Zoll (Generalzolldirektion): Notwendige Unterlagen, accessed 17 Nov 2026
  • Europäische Kommission, Generaldirektion Steuern und Zollunion: Generalised System of Preferences (GSP) (Abschnitt vom 13.10.2026), accessed 18 Nov 2026
  • Europäische Kommission, Access2Markets: GSP system updates for the period 2026–2028 (01.12.2025), accessed 18 Nov 2026
  • Europäische Kommission: The EU-India trade agreement (zuletzt aktualisiert 11.09.2026), accessed 18 Nov 2026

Import into the Emirates and Saudi Arabia

What charges apply on import into the Emirates?

The standard customs duty rate in the member states of the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) is five percent. It is based on the CIF value, the value of the goods including insurance and freight.

Most goods are charged five percent duty in the Emirates. On top comes VAT of five percent. Some product groups have other rates or are duty-free; the tariff number decides.

Source
  • Germany Trade & Invest (GTAI): Zollbericht Vereinigte Arabische Emirate: Einfuhrabgaben (Stand 28.07.2025), accessed 16 Nov 2026

What charges apply on import into Saudi Arabia?

Saudi Arabia levies 15 percent VAT on all imported goods, regardless of the tariff number, the duty rate or a duty exemption. In the worked example of the tax authority ZATCA it is calculated on the CIF value.

Customs duty comes on top. It depends on the tariff number of the goods. No official overview of the rates is available for this site; ask the importer for the rate for your goods before shipping.

Importers must also register their products on the SABER platform of the Saudi standards authority SASO.

Sources
  • ZATCA (Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority, Saudi-Arabien): Guideline on Imports and Exports under VAT Provision, 2. Ausgabe, Mai 2026, accessed 16 Nov 2026
  • SASO (Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization): Saudi Standards Obligates Importers to Register All Products on Saber Platform (03.01.2021), accessed 16 Nov 2026

What applies to cosmetics in Dubai?

In Dubai, cosmetic products may only be imported, advertised and sold if they are registered in the Montaji system of Dubai Municipality. The registration is valid for five years.

For the other Emirates and for Saudi Arabia there is no clear official source. Please clarify the requirements there with your importer.

Source
  • Dubai Municipality, Health & Safety Department: Technical Guidelines for Cosmetics and Personal Care Products, Version 2.1 (15.09.2025), accessed 16 Nov 2026
